# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Trackside Labs is for F1 fans and followers — people who care about the sport
and want a credible, readable forecast for each race weekend without needing to
be data scientists. They arrive around a specific event (an upcoming Grand Prix
or sprint), want to know what the model expects and how confident it is, and
leave. The interface has to reward a fluent motorsport reader while never
assuming they read calibration plots for a living.

Data is central, but it must stay user-friendly. The headline answer is always
plain and immediate; the depth sits *on demand*. A fan who just wants the
forecast gets it at a glance, and anyone who wants to dive deeper can pull up the
supporting evidence — and find it explained *with* the data, not asserted. The
design serves both readers from the same screen without punishing either.

## Product Purpose

Trackside Labs is an independent F1 2026 race-weekend prediction system. It
produces checkpointed qualifying and race forecasts and updates them as
practice, qualifying, sprint, and race evidence arrives across the regulation
reset. The project launched around April 2026 and its near-term focus is growth:
earning an audience and a following. Success is not a one-off accurate call; it
is becoming a *credible independent* motorsport-forecasting name that a growing
group of fans returns to each weekend, because its forecasts have earned trust
over a season with their accuracy on the record. The interface is central to
that: it has to convert a first-time visitor into someone who comes back.

## Positioning

The F1 forecast that knows how much to trust each signal. In a regulation-reset
year the hard part is not running the simulation — it is weighting stale
baselines against current-but-hidden testing pace against fresh-but-noisy Friday
practice. Every screen should reinforce that this system is disciplined about
what it believes and why.

## Brand Personality

Professional and engaging. Race-paced energy carried by a disciplined,
evidence-first core: the surface can move with motorsport heat and momentum, but
the numbers lead and the tone stays analytical — confident without being loud,
never hype. Professional earns the trust needed to build a following; engaging is
what makes a fan come back. Think a serious analytics instrument that happens to
be about racing, not a racing site with charts bolted on. Confidence without
false confidence: it states what it knows and shows the uncertainty around it.

## Anti-references

Not a betting or gambling site — no neon odds, no garish hype CTAs, none of the
sportsbook visual grammar. Not a generic SaaS dashboard — no cream-and-blue
template, no interchangeable hero-metric tiles or identical card grids, nothing
that reads as an anonymous AI-startup product. It must also stay visibly
independent: no mimicry of official series or team liveries and logos.

## Design Principles

Trust is earned by showing the work. Calibration, accuracy tracking, and honest
uncertainty are not appendices — they are the product's credibility, and the
interface should make them legible rather than hide them behind confident point
numbers.

Confidence without false confidence. Lead with intervals and probabilities over
single hard claims; the design should make a forecast's uncertainty feel like
rigor, not hedging.

Answer first, depth on demand. Serious modeling, made usable at race-weekend
speed. Every screen leads with a plain, immediate answer a fan can read at a
glance; the supporting evidence is always one deliberate step away, never forced
on the casual reader and never hidden from the curious one. And when depth is
opened, it is explained *with* the data — shown and reasoned, not just asserted.

Energy in service of the data. Motorsport pace and heat are welcome as accent
and motion, never as decoration that competes with the numbers. When energy and
legibility conflict, legibility wins.

Independent, and it shows. The look must read as an unaffiliated analytics
project on its own footing — credible in its own right, never borrowing the
authority of official F1 branding.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA: body text at ≥4.5:1 contrast (large text ≥3:1), visible
focus states on all interactive controls, keyboard navigability, and a
reduced-motion alternative for every animation. The dark-theme surface and
heat-orange accent must be checked against these thresholds rather than assumed
to pass.
